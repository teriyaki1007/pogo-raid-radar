Avocado Sushi Bake

4–6 servings ~25 min prep ~20 min cook / bake ~45 min total Quantities estimated

A warm deconstructed sushi casserole: seasoned short-grain rice pressed into a baking dish, topped with salmon, creamy avocado, nori strips, sesame, and orange/white drizzle sauces — scoopable, shareable, and weeknight-friendly.

Source note. Inspired by @cccooocccooo_1 Instagram reel. Exact amounts weren’t listed — proportions below are sensible home estimates matching the video’s assembly.
Approximate / not an official recipe. Sushi-bake style assembly reconstructed from the reel’s visuals. Adjust salmon, avocado, and sauces to taste.

Ingredients

Estimates for one ~20×20 cm (8×8 in) bake, 4–6 scoops.

Rice base

  • 300 g uncooked short-grain sushi rice (about 2 cups cooked yield after rinsing/cooking)
  • 3 Tbsp rice vinegar
  • 1 Tbsp sugar
  • ¾ tsp salt

Toppings

  • 250–300 g salmon (sashimi-grade for raw-style, or cooked/baked flaked salmon)
  • 2 ripe avocados, sliced or cubed
  • 2–3 nori sheets, cut into strips (or furikake / crumbled nori)
  • 1–2 Tbsp toasted sesame seeds (white and/or black)
  • Optional: thinly sliced cucumber, green onion, tobiko/masago

Sauces

  • Soy sauce or ponzu, for drizzling / dipping
  • White sauce: 3 Tbsp Kewpie / mayo + 1 tsp rice vinegar + splash of water (or cream cheese thinned)
  • Orange / spicy sauce: 2 Tbsp mayo + 1–2 tsp sriracha + ½ tsp honey or mirin (spicy mayo variant)
  • Optional eel (unagi) sauce or sweet soy glaze

Method

  1. Cook and season the rice. Rinse sushi rice until water runs clearer; cook per package. Warm, fold in vinegar, sugar, and salt. Spread into a lightly oiled baking dish and press into an even layer.
  2. Prep the salmon. For a warm bake: flake cooked salmon (season lightly with soy or salt). For a cooler finish: cube sashimi-grade salmon and keep chilled until assembly. Optional: toss cooked salmon with a spoon of spicy mayo.
  3. Layer. Scatter salmon over the rice. Add avocado slices/cubes. Tuck nori strips through the top (or sprinkle crumbled nori / furikake).
  4. Bake (optional warm finish). If using cooked salmon and you want it hot, bake at ~180°C (350°F) for 10–15 minutes until warmed through. Add avocado after baking if you prefer it fresh and green. For a raw-salmon style, skip the oven and serve the rice warm with cold toppings.
  5. Sauce and sesame. Drizzle white sauce and orange/spicy sauce in zigzags. Add soy or ponzu at the table. Finish with sesame seeds and green onion.
  6. Serve. Scoop into bowls or onto nori sheets like hand rolls. Eat warm or room-temp.

Tips

  • Press the rice firmly so scoops hold together.
  • Add avocado last if baking so it doesn’t brown or go mushy.
  • Use sashimi-grade fish only if serving raw; otherwise cook the salmon fully.
  • Sauces make it — taste and adjust spicy mayo heat before drizzling.
