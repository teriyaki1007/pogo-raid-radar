Carbonara

2 servings ~10 min prep ~15 min cook ~25 min total Quantities estimated

Classic Roman carbonara — crispy guanciale, silky egg-and-pecorino sauce, lots of black pepper, and spaghetti or tonnarelli. No cream. The emulsion comes from pasta water, heat control, and a fast toss off the flame.

Source note. Inspired by @_pietromino Instagram reel. Exact amounts weren’t listed — proportions below are classic Roman estimates matching the reel’s visuals (guanciale, eggs, hard cheese, pepper, pasta).
Approximate / not an official recipe. Classic home carbonara method with sensible amounts for 2 servings. No cream. Adjust cheese and pepper to taste.

Ingredients

Estimates for 2 servings.

  • 200–220 g spaghetti or tonnarelli
  • 100–120 g guanciale (or pancetta if needed), cut into batons / lardons
  • 2 large egg yolks + 1 whole large egg
  • 50–60 g finely grated Pecorino Romano (or ½ pecorino + ½ Parmigiano-Reggiano)
  • Freshly cracked black pepper, generously
  • Kosher salt, for the pasta water
  • Reserved pasta water (about ¾–1 cup), as needed

Method

  1. Boil the pasta. Bring a large pot of well-salted water to a boil. Cook spaghetti or tonnarelli until just shy of al dente. Reserve at least 1 cup of starchy pasta water before draining.
  2. Render the guanciale. While pasta cooks, place guanciale in a cold wide pan over medium heat. Cook, stirring occasionally, until fat renders and edges are golden and crisp — about 6–8 minutes. Keep the rendered fat in the pan; turn heat to low.
  3. Whisk the egg mixture. In a bowl, whisk egg yolks and whole egg with most of the grated cheese and a very generous grind of black pepper until thick and paste-like. (No cream.)
  4. Combine off heat. Remove the guanciale pan from heat. Add drained pasta to the pan and toss in the warm fat. Wait 20–30 seconds so the pan isn’t screaming hot, then pour in the egg–cheese mixture. Toss vigorously, adding splash after splash of hot pasta water until the sauce turns glossy and coats every strand without scrambling into curds.
  5. Finish and plate. Taste for salt (guanciale and pecorino are salty). Plate immediately; top with remaining cheese, more black pepper, and a few crisp guanciale pieces.

Tips

  • Heat control is everything — sauce forms with residual heat + pasta water, not a simmering pan.
  • If it tightens too much, loosen with more hot pasta water; if too thin, more cheese and a brief toss.
  • Pecorino is traditional and sharper; a blend with Parm softens the bite.
  • Guanciale is ideal; pancetta works in a pinch — avoid lean bacon if you can.
